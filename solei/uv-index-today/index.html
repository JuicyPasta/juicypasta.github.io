    What to do at each UV level

    UV index categories and the protection the WHO advises
    UV indexCategoryWhat to do
    0 to 2LowNo special protection needed. You can be outside safely.
    3 to 5ModerateProtection needed: shade around midday, and a shirt, sunscreen and a hat.
    6 to 7HighProtection needed, as for moderate. Without sunscreen, fair skin reaches its limit in under 20 minutes.
    8 to 10Very highExtra protection: stay out of the sun around midday and seek shade. A shirt, sunscreen and a hat are a must.
    11 or moreExtremeExtra protection, as for very high. At midday very fair skin reaches its limit in under 10 minutes.

    Source: World Health Organization, Global Solar UV Index: A Practical Guide (2002) and its questions on the UV index. The minutes are Solei's estimates for skin types II and I.

    The official UV forecasts

    This page is a quick look, not the official forecast. For that, go to your national service. Their numbers include the cloud forecast; the numbers here are the clear-sky model, the UV each hour would get if the sky stays clear.

    • Australia: the Bureau of Meteorology forecasts the UV index and the sun protection times for each town, and ARPANSA measures the UV live in the capital cities.
    • New Zealand: MetService and NIWA (now Earth Sciences New Zealand) publish the UV forecasts.
    • South Africa: the South African Weather Service (SAWS).

    Questions

    What is the UV index?

    It's the World Health Organization's scale for how strongly the sun burns at a place and time. It's 0 at night and rises with the sun: 0 to 2 is low, 3 to 5 moderate, 6 to 7 high, 8 to 10 very high and 11 or more extreme. The higher it is, the faster skin burns.

    What time is the UV highest today?

    Around solar noon, when the sun is highest. With daylight saving that's after 13:00 on the clock in Melbourne, Adelaide and Auckland, and closer to 12:00 in Brisbane and Perth, which don't use it. The page above gives the peak hour for your city. Early morning and late afternoon are much lower.

    When do I need sun protection?

    When the UV index is 3 or more. That's the WHO's advice and what Australia's sun protection times are based on: shade, a shirt, a hat, sunscreen and sunglasses. In an Australian or New Zealand summer it's usually 3 or more from mid morning to late afternoon, even on cool days, and thin cloud doesn't stop it.

    Do clouds lower the UV index?

    Thick cloud does, but thin or broken cloud lets most UV through and sometimes adds a little. That's why this page shows the clear-sky index, the most each hour can get, with the forecast cloud cover next to it. Water, sand and snow reflect UV and add more.

    How long can I stay in the sun today?

    It depends on your skin type and your sunscreen. For each day, this page gives the time to the safe limit without sunscreen at the peak hour, for all six skin types. With sunscreen, the UV tanning calculator works it out for your SPF and tells you when to reapply.

    Is this the official UV forecast?

    No. The official forecasts come from the Bureau of Meteorology and ARPANSA in Australia, MetService and NIWA in New Zealand and the South African Weather Service. Theirs include cloud. This page shows MET Norway's clear-sky model for anywhere in the world, worked out in your browser.

    Where does the data come from?

    From the forecast of MET Norway, Norway's weather service, which covers the whole world and publishes its data under CC BY 4.0. This page doesn't keep your location: it only sends MET Norway the coordinates rounded to about 1 km to ask for the forecast.
