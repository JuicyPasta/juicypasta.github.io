What UV index is best for tanning?

None. No UV level makes a tan safe. Skin tans and burns from the same UV, and a tan is the skin reacting to damage that's already done. The WHO advises sun protection from UV 3 up: shade around midday, clothes, a hat and sunscreen.

What the UV index changes is the minutes. Each step adds the same amount of sunburn-causing UV per minute, so UV 8 gets you to your limit in half the time UV 4 does. A higher index doesn't tan you better, it just gives you less time before you burn. Without sunscreen, going from UV 3 to UV 8 cuts the time to the safe limit from about 29 to 11 minutes for type I, 37 to 13 for type II, 44 to 16 for type III, 66 to 25 for type IV, 88 to 33 for type V and 148 to 55 for type VI.

The calculator above gives the minutes for your skin type and sunscreen. If you're out anyway: lower UV in the morning and late afternoon gives you more time than the midday peak, sunscreen adds more, turning over doesn't add any, and the limit counts for the whole day.

How sunscreen changes the time

Sunscreen counts as the square root of its SPF (SPF 30 counts as about 5.5×), because most people apply far less than the amount used in lab tests. It only counts until it's time to reapply: every 2 hours, or every 80 minutes when you swim or sweat, and right after a swim. SPF 50+ counts as SPF 60.

So the calculator never shows more than one reapply interval with sunscreen: if your limit lies further out, it says "2 h, then reapply" and how long the limit would take if you reapply on time. Reapplying keeps the protection going; it doesn't reset the limit, which counts for the whole day.

Questions

What UV index is best for tanning?

There isn't one. No UV level makes a tan safe: skin tans and burns from the same UV, and a tan is a sign of skin damage. The WHO advises protection from UV 3 up. A higher index only means fewer minutes before you burn; at UV 8, unprotected type II skin reaches its safe limit in about 13 min.

How long can I stay in the sun at UV 8?

Without sunscreen, at a steady UV 8, skin reaches its estimated safe limit after about 11 minutes for type I, 13 for type II, 16 for type III, 25 for type IV, 33 for type V and 55 for type VI. That's the time before a burn, not a tanning time. SPF 30 applied generously gives type III skin about 1 h 31 min, inside one 2-hour reapply window.

How long at UV 10?

Without sunscreen, at a steady UV 10, skin reaches its estimated safe limit after about 8 minutes for type I, 11 for type II, 13 for type III, 20 for type IV, 26 for type V and 44 for type VI. SPF 30 gives type III skin about 1 h 13 min. UV 10 is very high: the WHO advises avoiding the midday sun.

How long can I tan at UV 3 or UV 5?

Without sunscreen, at UV 3 the estimated safe limit comes after about 29 minutes for type I, 37 for type II, 44 for type III, 66 for type IV, 88 for type V and 148 for type VI. At UV 5 it's about 17 minutes for type I, 22 for type II, 26 for type III, 40 for type IV, 53 for type V and 88 for type VI.

Sources: WHO, the UV index and the Global Solar UV Index guide.

About the estimates

Safe sun time is how long it takes to reach two thirds of the dose that typically causes the first redness for your skin type. Each step of the UV index adds 1.5 J/m² of sunburn-weighted UV per minute, and the doses used for skin types I to VI are 200, 250, 300, 450, 600 and 1,000 J/m², the cautious low ends of the published ranges. This page assumes the UV index stays the same for the whole time; real UV rises toward midday and falls after it, which the Solei app follows along the forecast.

These are estimates for healthy skin. Altitude, snow, water and sand add extra UV. Some medicines and skin conditions make skin far more sensitive. Keep babies out of direct sun.
