The forecast

How does the forecast work?

Four times a day, the world's aviation forecast centres publish the World Area Forecast System: a global forecast of turbulence at the heights airliners fly, in three-hour steps. Jetcalm draws the shortest path between your two airports, works out where the aircraft should be at each minute from your departure time, and reads the forecast at that place, time and height. Climb and descent use the lower levels; the cruise uses typical cruising heights.

You see two things: expected, the forecast at a typical cruising height on your path, and could reach, the strongest value nearby and at neighbouring heights, because real flights don't follow the line exactly.

Do I need my flight number?

No. Jetcalm searches by airport, city or airport code. Enter the departure time from your ticket, in the departure airport's local time. If you also enter the arrival time, the timeline matches your flight's length; otherwise Jetcalm estimates it from the distance.

How far ahead can I look?

The official forecast covers the next 48 hours, and that is free. If your flight is further away, the app tells you the date your forecast opens. Jetcalm Pro adds an early outlook from 2 to 5 days ahead; it is an estimate made from a global weather model and is labelled as early, because it changes more.

Why did my forecast change?

Because the atmosphere's forecast changed. A new run arrives about every six hours, and each one knows more about the jet stream and storms than the last. A forecast for tomorrow evening is firmer than one for two days out, and the app's confidence line says which you are looking at. Small shifts are normal: a band of bumps that moves 100 km or arrives an hour later can change one part of your timeline while the overall picture stays the same.

My flight felt different from the forecast.

That happens. The forecast describes areas of the sky, not your exact aircraft. Pilots climb, descend or go around rough air using reports from the aircraft ahead, and small patches of turbulence are too fine for any global forecast to place. Larger aircraft also feel the same air less. Treat the timeline as "where bumps are more likely", and use the day-after check-in in the app to tell us how it went (your answer stays on your phone).

What do the levels mean?

LevelWhat it feels like in the cabin
CalmNothing to notice, or an occasional gentle wobble.
LightSmall bumps, like a car on a gravel road. Drinks stay in the cup. Very common.
ModerateClear bumps and some jolts. Drinks can spill and walking is awkward. The seatbelt sign is usually on.
StrongFirm jolts that press you against the belt. Loose items move. Service stops. Uncommon.
SevereAbrupt, large movements. Rare, and pilots plan routes to stay away from areas where it is forecast.

The levels come from a standard measure of rough air called EDR (eddy dissipation rate), sorted into bands for a medium-sized airliner. The words in the app are the full description for each level; tap any part of the timeline to read it.

What is the thunderstorm note?

When the forecast shows large storm clouds near your path at cruising time, the app says so. Pilots route around storms, which can add a few minutes.

Why is part of my timeline blank?

A gap means "no data", never "calm". It appears beyond the forecast's reach in time, or on the stretch of a polar route north or south of 75° latitude, which the published forecast maps do not cover.

Using the app

Does it work offline, in the air?

Yes. Every forecast you open or save is stored on your phone and opens in airplane mode, with the time its data is from. Open your forecast once before boarding to have the latest. The Learn cards and the bump meter also work with no connection. Getting a new forecast needs a connection.

What is the bump meter?

It uses your phone's motion sensor to show how much the aircraft moved up or down, in g, with the biggest bump of the last minute. Most bumps that feel large turn out to be small numbers. A phone on a tray table exaggerates, so take it as reassurance, not as a measurement.

The app says the data is delayed.

Jetcalm couldn't fetch a fresh forecast (no connection, or the source is late), so it shows the last one it has and its age. It updates by itself the next time you open it online.

I'm not getting alerts.

Alerts are part of Pro and are sent for saved trips: when your forecast opens, the evening before, three hours before, and when the forecast changes. Allow notifications for Jetcalm in your phone's Settings, and on Android leave background activity allowed for the app. Phones limit background refreshes to save battery, so an alert sometimes says only that your forecast is ready; tap it to load the latest.

What is free and what is Pro?

Free: unlimited forecasts inside the 48-hour window with the full timeline, route map and airport weather, one saved trip, offline viewing, the bump meter and Learn. Pro is optional, by the month, by the year or as a one-time lifetime purchase, and adds unlimited saved trips, alerts, the early outlook, the world map with a time slider, and the timeline at lower and higher cruising heights. The app shows your store's price before you buy.

How do I restore a purchase or cancel?

Restore: Settings in the app, then Restore Purchases, signed in to the same Apple or Google account. Cancel a monthly or yearly plan in your store account: on iPhone, Settings, your name, Subscriptions; on Android, Google Play, your profile, Payments and subscriptions. Refunds are handled by Apple or Google.
