What a pilot report is, and isn't. A pilot report (PIREP) is what one crew felt at one time and place, sent by radio and passed on by air traffic control. They are sparse and come mostly from North America; many flights never report, so an empty area doesn't mean smooth air. They reach this page up to an hour or so after the fact. And they depend on the aircraft: "moderate" in a small turboprop can be "light" in a big jet.

Live reports and the forecast: use both

Reports tell you what has happened; the forecast tells you what is expected. Clear-air turbulence, the kind at cruise height, doesn't show on weather radar, so a cluster of moderate reports is the best live sign that a patch of air is rough. Pilots use them the same way: to pick a smoother height or route. For a flight later today or tomorrow, the forecast is the better guide:

What the levels mean

ReportWhat it feels like in the cabin
SmoothThe crew reported no turbulence.
LightSmall bumps, like a car on a gravel road. Drinks stay in the cup. Very common.
ModerateClear bumps and some jolts. Drinks can spill and walking is awkward. The seatbelt sign is usually on.
SevereAbrupt, large movements; loose items fly. Rare, and controllers warn other crews so they can avoid the area.

"Light to moderate" and "moderate to severe" are reports between two levels; extreme reports, very rare, are drawn as severe.

About this tracker

Every hour this page's feed collects the pilot reports (PIREPs, and AIREPs from airliners over the oceans) published by the NOAA/NWS Aviation Weather Center for the last 3 hours, and keeps every report that says something about turbulence, including "smooth". It shows the aircraft type when the crew gave one; it never shows call signs or tail numbers.

Turbulence is uncomfortable, but it is not dangerous to the aircraft: airliners are built for far more than they meet in flight. Most injuries happen to people who aren't belted in, so keep your seatbelt fastened whenever you are seated. This tracker is for passengers; it is not a flight-planning tool, and Jetcalm is not affiliated with any airline.
