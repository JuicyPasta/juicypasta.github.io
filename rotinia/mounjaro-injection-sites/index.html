Mounjaro injection sites: where to inject, and is there a best place?

Short answer: inject Mounjaro under the skin of your stomach (abdomen) or thigh, or have another person inject it in the back of your upper arm. Change (rotate) the site with each weekly injection; the same area is fine if the spot is different, and the KwikPen instructions say at least 2 inches from the belly button. If you also take insulin, inject them separately, never mixed: the same body area is fine, but not right next to each other.

The planner below keeps the rotation for you: tap the site you used last and it shows the next one.

Where is the best place to inject Mounjaro?

The Mounjaro label doesn't name one best place. It lists the stomach (abdomen) and thigh, plus the back of the upper arm when another person gives the injection, and says "Similar exposure was achieved with subcutaneous administration of tirzepatide in the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm" (exposure is how much of the medicine reaches the bloodstream). The pen instructions say: "Your healthcare provider can help you choose the injection site that is best for you."

  • Rotate: "Change (rotate) your injection site each week. You may use the same area of your body but be sure to choose a different injection site in that area."
  • Avoid: the vial instructions say "Do not inject where the skin has pits, is thickened, or has lumps" and "Do not inject where the skin is tender, bruised, scaly or hard, or into scars or damaged skin." The KwikPen instructions keep stomach injections "at least 2 inches from the belly button".
  • Under the skin only: "Do not inject MOUNJARO into a muscle (intramuscularly) or vein (intravenously)." (Medication Guide)
  • With insulin: the same body area is fine, "but not right next to each other."

Where the Mounjaro label says to inject

Section 2.2 of the Mounjaro prescribing information: "Inject MOUNJARO subcutaneously in the abdomen, thigh, or have another person inject MOUNJARO in the back of the upper arm." It also says "Rotate injection sites with each dose" and to give it "once weekly, any time of day, with or without meals." The Medication Guide puts it in plain words: "Inject MOUNJARO under the skin (subcutaneously) of your stomach (abdomen), thigh, or another person should inject in the back of the upper arm."

Mounjaro and insulin: can you inject in the same area?

Yes, as two separate injections. The prescribing information says: "When using MOUNJARO with insulin, administer as separate injections and never mix. It is acceptable to inject MOUNJARO and insulin in the same body region, but the injections should not be adjacent to each other." The Medication Guide: "You may give an injection of MOUNJARO and insulin in the same body area (such as your stomach area), but not right next to each other." The vial instructions add: "Do not inject MOUNJARO in the same injection site used for other medicines."

If you use both, ask your doctor or pharmacist how to plan the two sets of injection sites.

How often to rotate Mounjaro sites

Every week. The pen instructions: "Change (rotate) your injection site each week. You may use the same area of your body but be sure to choose a different injection site in that area." The vial instructions give the reason: to reduce the risk of "lipodystrophy (pits in skin or thickened skin) and localized cutaneous amyloidosis (skin with lumps) at the injection sites."

How to inject Mounjaro

Mounjaro comes as a single-dose pen, single-dose and multi-dose vials, and the KwikPen (4 weekly doses). Here is the gist of the two pen instructions; follow the full Instructions for Use for yours, and ask your pharmacist to walk you through it.

  • Pen: "Do not remove the Pen from your skin until you hear the second loud click." The instructions add: "Some people may hear a soft click right before the second loud click."
  • KwikPen: "A drop of medicine on the needle tip is normal. It will not affect your dose." If you see blood, "lightly press the injection site with gauze or a cotton ball."
  • Vials: a new syringe and needle each time; the needle stays in the skin for at least 5 seconds.

Nothing here is about how much to take or when to change it: your prescriber decides that, and the dose is set on the label of the Mounjaro you were given.
