Ozempic injection sites: where to inject, and is there a best place?

Short answer: inject Ozempic under the skin of your stomach (abdomen), thigh or upper arm, once a week on the same day. Use a different spot each week, even within the same area. If you also take insulin, give them as separate injections, never mixed; the same body area is fine, but not right next to each other.

The planner below keeps the rotation for you: tap the site you used last and it shows the next one.

Where is the best place to inject Ozempic?

The Ozempic label doesn't name one best place. It lists three areas, the stomach (abdomen), thigh and upper arm, and says "Similar exposure is achieved with subcutaneous administration of semaglutide in the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm" (exposure is how much of the medicine reaches the bloodstream). The prefilled syringe instructions say: "Your healthcare provider can help you choose the injection site that is best for you."

  • Rotate: "Change (rotate) your injection site with each injection. Do not use the same site for each injection." (Medication Guide)
  • Avoid: "Do not inject OZEMPIC into a muscle (intramuscularly) or vein (intravenously)." The syringe instructions keep stomach injections "at least 2 inches from the belly button" and say the back of the upper arm is "only with help from someone else".
  • With insulin: the same body area is fine, "but not right next to each other."

Where the Ozempic label says to inject

Section 2 of the Ozempic prescribing information: "Inject OZEMPIC subcutaneously in the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm." It says to "use a different injection site each week when injecting in the same body region," and to give Ozempic "once weekly, on the same day each week, at any time of the day, with or without meals." The label adds that "similar exposure is achieved" with injections in the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm; it names no best area.

The Medication Guide: "Change (rotate) your injection site with each injection. Do not use the same site for each injection." The Ozempic single-dose prefilled syringe instructions spell out the areas: "Stomach area (abdomen) at least 2 inches from the belly button", thighs, and the "Back of upper arm (only with help from someone else)", and "Avoid using the exact same spot as the last time between injections."

Ozempic and insulin in the same area

From the prescribing information: "When using OZEMPIC with insulin, instruct patients to administer as separate injections and to never mix the products. It is acceptable to inject OZEMPIC and insulin in the same body region, but the injections should not be adjacent to each other." The Medication Guide: "You may give an injection of OZEMPIC and insulin in the same body area (such as your stomach area), but not right next to each other."

How to inject Ozempic with the pen

The Ozempic pen holds several weekly doses and uses a separate NovoFine Plus needle ("NovoFine® Plus 32G 4 mm disposable needles are included with your OZEMPIC pen"). The main points of its Instructions for Use; read the full instructions, and ask your doctor or pharmacist to show you.

  1. A new needle every time: "Always use a new needle for each injection." "Do not attach a new needle to your pen until you are ready to give your injection."
  2. Flow check, new pens only: "Check the OZEMPIC flow before the first injection with each new pen only." Turn the dose selector to the flow check symbol, hold the pen needle up and press until the counter shows 0; a drop should appear at the needle tip.
  3. Select your dose as your prescriber told you. "Do not set the dose by counting the number of clicks you hear."
  4. Inject: insert the needle as your healthcare provider showed you, keep the dose counter visible, and "Press and hold down the dose button until the dose counter shows 0." Then "Count 6 seconds while keeping the dose button pressed." Remove the needle, then release the button.
  5. After: "Always dispose of the needle after each injection", into a sharps container, and store the pen without a needle on it.

"Do not share your OZEMPIC pen with other people, even if the needle has been changed." If blood appears, press lightly with a gauze pad or cotton ball; don't rub.

Nothing here is about how much to take or when to change it: your prescriber decides that, and the dose is set on the label of the Ozempic you were given.
